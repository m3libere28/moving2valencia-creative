Phase 1 — Decision & Research
12–6 months before your move
0/10
+
Choose your visa type Urgent
EU/EEA citizens: free movement. Non-EU: Digital Nomad Visa (€2,646/month income req.), Non-Lucrative Visa (€28,800/year), or Work Permit. Each has different document requirements.
Research Valencia neighbourhoods
Ruzafa (expat hub, bars, market), El Carmen (historic, bohemian), Benimaclet (village feel), El Cabanyal (beach, €600-900/mo), Malvarrosa (seafront, quieter), Campanar (families, modern).
Set your monthly budget Plan Early
Average 2024 costs: 1-bed apartment €700–1,100/mo, 3-bed €1,200–1,800/mo. Utilities ~€120/mo. Groceries €300–500/mo for a family. International school fees €700–1,500/mo per child.
Book a scouting trip to Valencia Recommended
Visit your preferred neighbourhoods in person. Walk the markets, eat at the restaurants, feel the pace. Nothing replaces being there.
Research school options for children
Key schools: Caxton College (British), American School, Deutsche Schule, Colegio Alemán, Lyric (IB). Public school enrolment via Conselleria d'Educació. Concertado schools are semi-private at low cost.
Understand the NIE timeline Critical
The NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) is required to sign a lease, open a bank account, buy a car, and more. EU citizens can self-apply; Non-EU must apply at a consulate before arriving in many cases. Process can take 4–12 weeks.
Get documents apostilled in your home country Start Early
Birth certificates, marriage certificate, criminal record, university degrees, and children's school records may all require apostilles. In the UK/US this takes 2–8 weeks and must be done before you leave.
Book a free consultation with Christa
A 15-minute call can save months of confusion. Christa will tell you exactly what you need, what order to do it in, and what mistakes to avoid.
Notify your employer / plan remote work If Applicable
If working remotely, confirm your company's policy on working from Spain. Tax implications apply after 183 days of Spanish residence — consult a gestoría early.
Begin Spanish language learning Recommended
Even basic Spanish transforms your daily experience. Apps: Duolingo, Babbel. In Valencia, Valencian (Catalan variant) is also spoken — some official documents are in both languages.

Phase 2 — Legal & Documents
6–3 months before your move
0/10
+
Apply for NIE at Spanish consulate (non-EU) Critical
Non-EU nationals must apply for an NIE at the Spanish consulate in their home country before relocating, as part of the long-stay visa application. EU nationals can apply on arrival at a Comisaría in Valencia.
Apply for long-stay visa (non-EU) Critical
Digital Nomad Visa, Non-Lucrative Visa, or Golden Visa — apply at your local Spanish embassy. Processing: 1–3 months. Required documents include criminal record, health insurance, proof of income/funds.
Get certified translations of key documents Required
All non-Spanish documents used in official processes require certified Spanish translation by a sworn translator (traductor jurado). Cost: €30–80 per document.
Arrange private health insurance Required for non-EU visa
Required for most Spanish visas. Providers: Adeslas, Sanitas, Cigna, Allianz. Average family cost: €80–250/mo. Must cover all risks with no geographical limitations and minimum €30,000 coverage.
Organise international money transfer
Wise, Revolut, or local bank wire. You'll need funds in Spain for the rental deposit (typically 1–2 months) plus first month's rent before your Spanish account is open. Minimum recommend: €3,000–5,000 liquid on arrival.
Check driving licence requirements 6-month window
Non-EU licences can be driven for 6 months after becoming Spanish resident. After that, you must exchange (UK, US, Canada) or take the full Spanish test. Book the DGT exchange appointment well in advance — slots are scarce.
Research schools and begin applications International schools
International schools often have waiting lists of 6–18 months. Contact them as early as possible. Public school enrolment via Conselleria d'Educació — application periods are typically March–April for September start.
Decide on shipping vs. buying new
Spanish apartments are typically furnished. Shipping costs for a 40ft container from the UK: ~€3,000–5,000. From the US: €8,000–15,000. Many expats ship only irreplaceable items and buy new locally at IKEA, El Corte Inglés, or MediaMarkt.
Register with your home country's consulate Recommended
Register as a citizen abroad with your home country's foreign ministry. Useful for emergencies, voting, and official communications.
Close or transfer accounts in home country
Subscriptions, bank accounts, pension contributions, national insurance/social security registrations. Some accounts (like UK ISAs) must remain UK-resident. Consult a tax adviser.

Phase 3 — Home Finding
3–1 months before your move
0/10
+
Confirm neighbourhood priority list
Cross-reference commute (if applicable), school location, expat community, budget, and lifestyle preferences. Visiting in person during a scouting trip is strongly recommended before committing.
Set up portal alerts (Idealista, Fotocasa) Do This Now
The Valencia rental market is competitive. Good apartments in Ruzafa and El Carmen are often rented within 24–48 hours of listing. Alerts must be instant. Budget: €800–1,400/mo for a well-located 2-bed.
Prepare rental dossier Required
Landlords require: NIE (or application receipt), 3 months bank statements, proof of income (payslips or self-employment), passport copy. Some require an aval (guarantor) or additional deposit (up to 3 months).
Book virtual property viewings
Request video walkthroughs from agents. Check: natural light (north-facing flats are dark), noise level, lift if high floor, storage, heating/AC (not all Spanish flats have central heating — essential in winter), parking if needed.
Understand Spanish lease law (LAU) Read Before Signing
Under LAU 2019: minimum 5-year lease term for individuals (7 for companies). You can leave after 6 months with 30 days notice. Annual increases capped by CPI. Deposit: legally 1 month (though landlords often request 2).
Review lease contract before signing Critical
Have a Spanish-speaking adviser review any contract before you sign. Check: who pays community fees and IBI (property tax), what's included in utilities, pet policy, and early termination clauses.
Arrange temporary accommodation for first weeks Recommended
Even if you have a flat lined up, book a backup. Lease signing often happens 24–48 hours before key handover. Airbnb, aparthotel, or extended-stay hotel for 1–2 weeks.
Research internet providers Plan Ahead
Main providers in Valencia: Movistar, Orange, Vodafone, MásMóvil. Average cost: €30–50/mo for fibre. Installation can take 2–3 weeks — book before you arrive if your landlord allows.
Book removals / international freight
If shipping belongings: book 6–8 weeks in advance. For smaller moves, consider a man-and-van service or shipping boxes via Eurosender/Transglobos. Customs documentation required for non-EU shipments.
Get home contents insurance quote Recommended
Spanish renters can take out their own contenido (contents) insurance separately from the landlord's continente (building) policy. Average cost: €150–300/year. Compare at AXA, Mapfre, or Línea Directa.

Phase 4 — Arrival Week
First 7 days in Valencia
0/12
+
Register for empadronamiento Do First
The empadronamiento (municipal registration) is done at your local Junta Municipal. Required: passport, rental contract, and in some districts, landlord's authorisation. Opens access to public services, schools, and health system. Free.
Book NIE / TIE appointment (EU citizens) Do First Week
EU citizens apply at the Oficina de Extranjería or Comisaría de Policía. Appointments released on ciudadaniaexterior.inclusion.gob.es — often gone within minutes of release. Use a service or Christa's team to secure one faster.
Open Spanish bank account Required for rent
Most landlords require rent by SEPA direct debit. BBVA and CaixaBank are expat-friendly. Sabadell has English-language services. You'll need: NIE, passport, empadronamiento. Non-residents may need a non-resident account first.
Get a Spanish SIM card
Prepaid: Orange, Vodafone, Movistar. For contract plans requiring NIE: Pepephone, Simyo, or MásMóvil are expat-friendly. Budget €10–25/mo for an unlimited data plan.
Transfer utilities to your name (if unfurnished)
Electricity (Iberdrola or Endesa), gas (Naturgy), and water (EMIVASA in Valencia). If furnished with utilities included — confirm this in writing in the lease.
Do a thorough property check-in inspection
Document every defect with photos and video before unpacking. Send to landlord within 48 hours in writing (WhatsApp is legally valid). This protects your deposit.
Register children for school If children
For public school: contact Conselleria d'Educació with empadronamiento and vaccination records. For international school: contact admissions with passport, previous school reports, and vaccination records.
Register with a local GP (médico de cabecera) If using public health
EU citizens with S1 form (or those paying into Spanish social security) access public healthcare. Register at your nearest Centro de Salud with empadronamiento and NIE. You'll be assigned a GP and health card (SIP card).
Get a Tarjeta Joven (youth card) Under 30s
Free card for under-30s offering discounts on transport, culture, and leisure across the Valencian Community. Apply at any Oficina Jove with empadronamiento and ID. Worth having.
Get your Metrovalencia transport card
The Abono Transporte card covers metro, bus (EMT), tram (Metrovalencia), and Cercanías trains within Valencia. Purchase at any metro station kiosk. €1.50/single, €33/mo unlimited.
Explore your neighbourhood on foot
Find your nearest mercado (Mercado Colón, Ruzafa Market, or Central Market), supermercado (Mercadona is the local favourite), pharmacy (farmacia — open late and staffed by pharmacists), and café con leche spot.
Join Valencia expat communities
InterNations Valencia, Meetup groups, Facebook: "British Expats Valencia", "Americans in Valencia", "Valencia Expats". Invaluable for recommendations, warnings, and making friends fast.

Phase 5 — First 6 Months
Ongoing tasks after arrival
0/11
+
Exchange driving licence (non-EU) 6-month deadline
UK, US, Canadian, and many other non-EU licences can be exchanged without a new test at the DGT. Book your appointment ASAP — 6 months passes very quickly. Required: NIE, empadronamiento, driving licence, medical certificate (from any clínica de conductores, ~€60).
Register as autónomo (self-employed) if applicable If self-employed
Must register with AEAT (tax authority) and TGSS (social security) within 30 days of starting activity. Monthly Social Security fee: €200–350 (reduced rate available for first 2 years: €80–200/mo). Recommend a gestoría to handle this.
Find a trusted gestoría Strongly Recommended
A gestoría is an administrative specialist who handles tax returns, autónomo registration, and bureaucratic paperwork. Essential for expats. Cost: €50–150/month for ongoing services, or per-task fees. Christa can introduce you to trusted gestores.
Declare Modelo 720 if applicable Deadline: 31 March
Spanish tax residents with foreign assets exceeding €50,000 (bank accounts, investments, property) must file Modelo 720 by 31 March each year. Non-compliance fines are extremely severe. Use a gestoría or tax adviser.
File Spanish income tax (IRPF) Deadline: 30 June
You become a Spanish tax resident after 183 days in Spain. First return covers the year of arrival (proportional). Filing period: April–June. Use a gestoría or Hacienda's Renta Web system (Spanish only).
Renew TIE card (non-EU residents) Every 1–5 years
Non-EU TIE cards must be renewed at the Oficina de Extranjería before expiry. Book your cita previa at least 2 months before the expiry date — appointments are scarce. Christa's team can monitor and book this for you.
Enrol children in after-school activities
Valencia has excellent football academies (including Valencia CF youth programme), swimming clubs, music schools, and language academies. The best programmes fill up fast — enquire within your first month.
Attend a Spanish conversation exchange
Free language exchange events happen every week across Valencia — cafés, community centres, and online groups. Intercambio Valencia (Facebook group) is the largest and most active.
Experience Las Fallas March
If you arrive before March: Las Fallas (March 15–19) is Valencia's defining festival — enormous papier-mâché sculptures, fireworks, and concerts. The mascletà (midday firecrackers in the Plaza del Ayuntamiento) is unmissable. Book accommodation for Fallas week well in advance.
Discover your local mercado
Valencia's covered markets are the heart of daily life. Mercado Central (UNESCO-listed, the largest in Europe), Mercado de Ruzafa, and Benimaclet market. Fresh produce, local cheese, jamón, and the best orange juice in the world.
