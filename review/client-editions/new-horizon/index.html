Chapter 4 · Your route to arrival

Four stages, planned around you.

Ask for help with one stage or all four. The service descriptions below come from the current site and will be confirmed with Christa.

01 · Plan

Before you move

A plan for your move that sets out what to arrange and in what order, fitted to your dates and your family.

Service: Pre-move planning

What this includes
  • A document checklist, including apostilles and translations where they’re needed
  • A timeline with milestones for each phase
  • Budget planning and a cost-of-living overview
  • A video consultation before you arrive

02 · Prepare

Papers and appointments

Appointments and paperwork organised so that steps which depend on each other happen in the right order.

Service: Legal & formalities support

What this includes
  • Booking NIE, TIE or EU registration certificate appointments
  • Empadronamiento (registering with the town hall)
  • Opening a Spanish bank account
  • Setting up private health insurance

03 · Arrive

A home and a school

Somewhere to live and a school for your children, chosen around your daily routines.

Services: Home finding · School search

What this includes
  • Talking through neighbourhoods based on how you live
  • Viewings in person or by video
  • Reviewing the lease before you sign
  • Setting up utilities and internet
  • School visits and introductions
  • Help with enrolment paperwork

04 · Settle

Life after the keys

Help with the rhythm of the city, and someone to ask once you’re living here.

Services: Intercultural coaching · Ongoing support

What this includes
  • A cultural orientation session
  • Introductions to clubs, groups and language classes
  • Reminders for renewals such as the TIE
  • An introduction to a gestoría for tax and admin

What the first call covers

  • Where you are now and when you’d like to move
  • Who is moving with you
  • Which stages you want help with
  • What Christa suggests doing first

Chapter 5 · A person beside you

One person who knows the way through.

Christa runs Moving2Valencia herself. She moved to Valencia with her own family and went through the same appointments, forms and first months you’re about to face.

Before relocation work, she spent years coordinating large stage productions across Europe, keeping many moving parts on schedule.

Chapter 6 · Somewhere to belong

Everyday Valencia, three ways.

General context to help you picture daily life. It isn’t a recommendation for any area; Christa talks through neighbourhoods with you based on your own priorities.

Use the arrow keys to move between views.

City rhythm

  • Later daysLunch is often the main meal, and dinner tends to be later than in much of northern Europe.
  • Las FallasEach March the city fills with giant monuments, music and fireworks for several days.
  • Two languagesValencian is co-official with Spanish, so you’ll see both on signs and official letters.

Outdoor life

  • The Turia gardensA park follows the old riverbed through the city, linking playgrounds, sports grounds and the City of Arts and Sciences.
  • City beachesUrban beaches such as Malvarrosa sit on the edge of the city, reachable by tram or bike.
  • The AlbuferaA natural park with a freshwater lagoon and rice fields lies just south of the city.

Family routines

  • School choicesOptions include public schools, concertado schools (state-funded, privately run) and private international schools.
  • WeekendsParks, the beach and long family lunches are a big part of many weekends.
  • Local lifeMany neighbourhoods have their own market, plaza and local festivities.

Before you write

Questions people ask first.

Do you give legal or immigration advice?

No. Christa organises appointments, paperwork and the practical steps. Questions about visa eligibility or tax are for a qualified lawyer or gestor, and she can introduce you to one.

Can you help before we arrive?

Yes. Planning, documents, and home and school searches can start while you’re still abroad, by video call.

Do I need to speak Spanish?

No. You can talk with Christa in English, and she helps with the appointments where Spanish or Valencian is used.

How much does it cost?

It depends on which stages you need. Christa explains the options and prices on the first call, with scope and fees discussed personally.
